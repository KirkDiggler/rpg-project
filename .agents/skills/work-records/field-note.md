# Field note: the readable story afterward

Write the retrospective after the investigation it describes. The whole feature
need not be finished: a completed investigation can end with a decision, a rejected
approach or an unresolved question. If the investigation is still unfolding,
capture a journal entry now and keep article possibilities as notes.

## Build the account from the record

Read the journal, relevant ADRs and current issue/PR evidence. Confirm what was
actually exercised, accepted and shipped; historical plans are not proof of any
of those. Follow the existing site's voice and neighboring articles without
rewriting their history or assuming their implementation claims are current.

Organize the article around a question a reader can follow:

1. **Need:** whose problem prompted the work, and what experience were they seeking?
2. **Starting point:** what existed, what we believed and what we did not know.
3. **Investigation:** the examples, observations and alternatives that mattered.
4. **Turn:** what changed our understanding, including dead ends and corrections.
5. **Outcome:** what was decided or learned, its costs and its limits.
6. **Next:** what remains unanswered or what later delivery will test.

Use only the parts the actual story needs. Keep an evidence trail with links to
the journal, decision and implementation records. Select the important moments;
a field note is not a transcript, run log or copied issue checklist.

## Voice and claims

Share the work; do not turn it into an employability pitch. Use the operator's
stated perspective and recognizable language. First-person drafts must stay
within supplied experience and intent, with the operator reviewing the voice.
Do not invent feelings, quotes or motives to connect paragraphs.

Preserve uncertainty rather than retrofitting inevitability. An example passing
is not the game shipping. Concurrent changes do not prove which one caused an
improvement. A negative finding can be the outcome; the article need not be a
success story. Distinguish measured results from expectations and future work.

## Placement and publication

Keep the draft in an agreed source location. Inspect the owning site's publishing
workflow before choosing it: some source paths deploy automatically when merged.
Drafting is not authorization to deploy or merge into an auto-published path.
A repository-only draft is still public when the repository is public.

Existing `story` overview pages are living architectural/delivery views, updated
as slices move. This retrospective is a different artifact; link it rather than
replacing that overview or freezing its delivery state into a competing tracker.

Before publishing, verify links and factual claims, check the rendered page when
applicable, follow the repository's documentation gates, and obtain the operator's
publication direction. No Pages changes are required merely to keep a journal.
