Before anything else

The record lies about its own dates

Roughly ten journey docs and twelve ADRs carry internal dates that git contradicts — almost all of them a 01-for-07 month typo with the day-of-month intact, plus four that are off by a year and one off by seven months. Publish git dates only.

There's a detail in this worth keeping for the story itself: from ADR-0030 onward, in 2026-05-30, every in-file date is finally accurate — the record started telling the truth about itself at roughly the same moment it started quoting its author by name. And the project documents its own unreliability rather than quietly fixing it. DECISIONS.md carries a section headed "Status fields are unreliable": "Most ADRs still read 'Proposed' or carry no status, including several that shipped years of code. Treat the status line as unmaintained."

Act one

July 2025 — four dragons on a blank map

The founding month. Twenty journey documents and nineteen ADRs in four weeks. The project stops building a game and starts building the machine underneath one, and discovers that the hard part is deciding what the machine is not allowed to know.

2025-07-03→ 07-05

Four days that open the map. The very first journey doc names four unknowns as sea monsters, admits "we've been glossing over this" about where state lives, and one commitment inside it becomes the spine of the next year: game-specific mechanics get injected from rulebook modules; the core provides hooks, never implementations.

  • 07-03 "Our event bus isn't just for conditions — it's the nervous system of the entire game." They set out to answer "what is a condition?" and found the question was too small: conditions, features, equipment, spells and terrain are all the same shape. 003-event-participant-ecosystem
  • 07-03 The ConcentrationManager is torn out. A D&D-specific manager had already shipped. "This felt too rulebook-specific." Concentration turned out to be one of six relationship kinds, and a generic RelationshipManager replaced it — at a cost the ADR states plainly. 004-conditions-system · ADR-0003, ADR-0004
  • 07-04 An Effect base class is proposed and refused. The temptation was exactly 72 duplicated lines. "Premature abstraction is dangerous — we almost created unnecessary complexity." The same doc catches a live bug in design: Bless grants +1d4, which must be re-rolled every attack, not frozen as +3 when the spell landed. 005-effect-composition · ADR-0005
  • 07-05 ProcessShortRest() is called "a red flag." A D&D rule had been written onto a generic resource pool. The first fix — events, but with the rest types still hardcoded — fails too. Two visible strikes before the answer: the pool never learns what a rest is. Dawn restoration, milestone recovery and a spell that refills an ally all fall out for free. 010-generic-event-patterns · ADR-0006/0007
2025-07-16→ 07-21

The ambition gets written down all at once. On a single day — 17 July — four documents land, including both the always-on Discord world and a no-GM spotlight system that watches who hasn't had a moment lately.

  • 07-16 Hexes are secretly three-dimensional. Hex math gets easy the moment you stop pretending hexes are a 2D grid. The same doc explains, in one formula, why D&D diagonals cost the same as straight lines. 012-spatial-module-design · ADR-0008
  • 07-17 Grimsby is invented. A vision doc mocks up a Discord transcript for a wandering merchant with a two-day timer, an autumn festival five days out, and rain that adds a day to travel. Thirteen months later half of this shipped — bigger than dreamed. Grimsby did not. 016-mmo-inspired-persistent-world-vision
  • 07-17 The multi-room orchestrator is declared complete — then found unsafe. Comprehensive docs, full test coverage, the 100-room performance target met, every box ticked. A review then finds unsynchronised maps, a leaked event subscription for every room ever added, and iterator invalidation during removal. "Infrastructure code requires higher standards than application code." 013-room-orchestrator-vision · ADR-0009
  • 07-18 "Patterns are algorithms, not data." Prefab rooms get costed at 6 shapes × 10 patterns = 60 files and killed before building. What ships is ~6KB of shape boundaries and zero pattern files. 015-environment-generation-design-evolution · ADR-0011
  • 07-18 An AI assistant's first instinct was to silence the linter. "I initially tried to suppress linting warnings with #nosec comments without fully understanding their purpose." The genuinely good insight underneath: for procedural generation, the "insecure" random number generator is the correct one, because you need the same seed to rebuild the same dungeon. 016-ai-assistant-code-quality-lessons
  • 07-20 An approved ADR is torn up whole, one day after it was written. A review found it had quietly turned the toolkit into a content management system — caching tiers, storage, normalisation schemas. The commit is titled "after sanity checking": 564 lines changed, 241 deleted, and the title of the decision itself replaced. This is the earliest firing of the reflex that thirteen months later calls a module name "a structural lie" — and the only reversal in the whole corpus caught by reading rather than by shipping. The filename is still a fossil of the decision that lost. In the same doc: a module renamed from experiences/ to orchestrators/ — not because of a bug, but because two humans kept mishearing it as "experience points" in conversation. 018-content-architecture-breakthrough · ADR-0018, rewritten 2025-07-21
  • 07-29 Type safety becomes a wall. Hard-typed class constants made the Artificer impossible to add. Kirk reframes it: "so this is not just data we want to inject but behaviour too." The resolution — the constants and a registry were never actually in conflict — was never built. There is still no Artificer. 020-extensible-registry-system · ADR-0019
Act two · the hinge

August 11–18, 2025 — eight days

Nineteen journey documents in eight days, and the filenames hid it completely: docs numbered 021–027 and 040–043 were written in the same week. It opens on the fourth attempt at the feature system and closes with a 2000-line event system becoming 500.

Seven documents, and not one of them decides anything. Every hedge, no commitments. Two of them contradict each other outright — one wants strict typing everywhere, another trades it away on purpose — and nobody notices for five days. This is the day the first unlock is quietly written down.

Same throughput as Tuesday, opposite register. Every document decides. The measured difference across the week: on 08-12, across four documents, the phrase "we need" appears zero times. On 08-17 it appears eleven times, and the hedges drop to zero.

  • 00:54 Three rulesets, one engine. Act three

Autumn 2025 — the first time anyone can see it

One isolated document in September, seven weeks of nothing, then five documents in seventeen days — three of them committed after midnight.

2025-09-28Sun
  • 14:52 The wall, and three failed escapes. A circular import you cannot escape by moving files. Three attempts named and failed — and the third, a separate events package, is the eventual answer, rejected here because he couldn't yet see where it sat in the hierarchy. The reframe is a question about purpose, and the answer needs no code at all: "Events describe what happened, not what exists." Shipped in the same commit as a working rage feature. 044-events-carry-actions-not-objects

Act four

May 2026 — coming back

What breaks the silence is not a design. It's an audit.

2026-05-03

The method is grep, run against a named working state, with a "verification commands" section so a reviewer can re-run every claim. That is not how you write about a system you currently hold in your head — it's how you write about one you're re-deriving from source. It has a section titled "Things I discovered off-script." It uses the word "surprising" twice, about its own repository. And it returns a verdict on its author's stated belief about his own architecture:

"Kirk's framing of them as both being 'Actions under the hood' is the audit's most material correction."
PARTIALLY TRUE — needs correction. 049-rpg-api-toolkit-usage-audit

It also finds six documented, first-class modules that the game server imports zero times, and a deprecation comment announcing a migration that nobody can now say is in flight or stalled. That is what five months leaves behind, in one sentence.

Act six

August 2026 — standing at altitude

Eleven ADRs in seventeen days, four journey documents in five. The month opens by reading three months of largely autonomous work end to end and concluding that every individual change was defensible and the whole thing was the wrong shape.

The whole life of the thing being rebuilt is 110 days, and it begins the week the silence ends. The encounter module's first commit is 2026-05-06 — three days after the audit. Its last is 2026-08-24, "retire the top-level legacy encounter module." Skeleton to retirement: 120 commits, ~18k lines of production Go, running the live game the whole time.

2026-08-31

The living world ships. Every action becomes a fact; facts fold into each witness's present; predicates watch the folds. Proven in 228 tests. What comes out of an attempt is not "success" — it is a fact with an audience. Stealth stops being a mechanic bolted on and becomes authorship of your own audience. Per-witness belief cost zero lines of special-case code, because it isn't a feature of the system; it is the system.

"The streamer's camera sees everything; each character sees only their own feed."

The most interesting thing the calendar bought. In the month his game finally became something you could look at, he did not let visibility set the agenda — 051 asks outright for room to build something that would not be useful for a while, and closes "nothing forces a date; usefulness is allowed to arrive later. That's the point." And then, four days later, every decision in 053 is validated against the real thing: the benchmark world is the actual game's tomb, three rooms and six members; the unit of work is a player's click; the load model is a six-player party clicking once a second. He bought freedom on shape and refused it on cost. Four days apart, and not in conflict.

Honesty inventory

What reversed, and what was never built

Both lists belong on the published page. They are what make everything else on it credible.

Reversals on the record

  • ADR-0036 → ADR-0041, nine days apart. A critical-hit rule adopted deliberately, then reversed — not on preference but on structure. Recorded exemplarily: dated supersession note, body preserved intact, "retained as historical context."
  • ADR-0021 → 0028 → 0045, a twelve-month arc. Actions go from internal pattern, to first-class self-subscribing objects, to inert data with no behaviour at all. An earlier ADR had recorded pulling back from abstracting everything to "actions" — it was right about premature and wrong about never.
  • ADR-0034 superseded by not happening. The diagnosis was right, the prescribed surgery was overtaken, and the module was deleted instead.
  • ADR-0025 superseded by a non-ADR. The architectural record moved to another repository. Anyone reconstructing decisions from this folder hits a signposted dangling pointer.
  • The index reversed itself. A hand-maintained ADR list "drifted to naming 7 of 37 and nothing failed" — so it was replaced by a file enforced in CI. The project changed its own documentation process after noticing an unenforced list silently stops being true.

And the irony worth building a section around: ToData came out of the least confident day of the whole week — the day of unpicked options and unresolved questions — and it is still load-bearing across the entire codebase fourteen months later. The idea he was excited about that week is the one that didn't ship.

The proposal

Eight beats

The spine is two of Kirk's own sentences thirteen months apart — "I am an architect and not an application developer" and "what no agent ever did was stand at altitude and ask whether the shape was still right." Underneath every beat, the same pattern: build the specific thing, find it's too specific, back it out, and the answer that replaces it is always smaller and one level up.

  1. The halfling with the tree trunk

    2026 · full width, no preamble

    Fourteen months to be able to take this screenshot, and almost none of it went into the halfling.

  2. Four dragons on a blank map

    July 2025

    The founding month opens by naming what nobody had decided yet. A D&D-specific concentration manager gets torn out. ProcessShortRest() is called a red flag. The lesson that repeats for a year: the toolkit provides mechanism, the game provides meaning.

  3. The week

    August 11–18, 2025 · told hour by hour

    Opens on the fourth attempt. ToData lands on the least confident day. Rage deadlocks Saturday at 23:22. Sunday at 00:54 the human overrules the machine and half-articulates the answer in a broken sentence. Monday at 22:15: you had me at.

  4. The first time you can see it

    Autumn 2025

    A +2 appears, itemised, in a combat breakdown. The era ends with four commits in thirty minutes on a Wednesday night, and one idea written at 1:26 AM that never got built.

  5. The silence

    January – April 2026

    Ten merged PRs in February. Nothing written down at all. What breaks it is an audit whose verdict on its own author's mental model is partially true — needs correction.

  6. The art arrives

    July 18, 2026

    457 merged PRs the following month, against a trough of ten. You can only iterate on what you can look at — and the engine didn't have to change to accept the halfling, which is the entire argument for the four beats above it.

  7. Standing at altitude

    August 2026

    Reading three months of your own project end to end and concluding it's the wrong shape. "That is not a module. That is the game loop, living in the toolshed." A B+ thrown away clean, and seven orthogonal axes in its place.

  8. The living world, and Grimsby

    August 31, 2026 → now

    Collective memory, relationship networks, long-running quests and shared goals — all dreamed in month one, all shipped bigger than dreamed. Weather, festivals and the wandering merchant did not. Close on failure at scale manufactures act two, then back to the halfling.
