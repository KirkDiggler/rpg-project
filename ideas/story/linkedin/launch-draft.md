My time at GOAT has ended.

Seven years is the longest I have ever stayed at one company. That is a testament to how much room I had to build there. I was allowed to notice recurring problems and make the things nobody had thought to ask for yet. Once delivered, they became part of how people worked.

I had the right people around me, too. My teammate is the best. The infrastructure team are my kind of people, and they had done the hard work of making building genuinely easy before I got there. I got accustomed to taking a service from nothing to running in staging in under thirty minutes, already hooked into whichever AWS resources it needed.

This is usually the part where someone says they are excited about whatever comes next. I am fortunate enough to know what I am excited about.

Many of the people reading this knew me when I worked in games. The industry moves people around so thoroughly that, eventually, former teammates and friends seem to work at almost every studio.

I had been out of video games for about five years when the itch came back. Evidently, I spent the next fourteen months building one.

It started as a Discord adventure bot. It became a reusable rules engine, a game server, and a shared 3D dungeon. Most of that work was invisible until a halfling carrying a club roughly as long as he is walked into it.

For the first time, the game has my full attention. Ideas I had treated as distant possibilities suddenly feel attainable.

I wrote down the whole story, from the early pull request that accidentally told me what kind of builder I am, the architectural breakthroughs, the work I threw away, the four months of silence, the dice on the dungeon floor, and why games keep calling me back:

https://kirkdiggler.github.io/rpg-project/

I want to share more of the pieces next: the week barbarian rage deadlocked the event bus and changed the architecture, why the encounter turned out to be one tool among many, and eventually the living world built from facts and witnesses.

For now, the club is roughly one and a half halflings long. I am very happy with it.
