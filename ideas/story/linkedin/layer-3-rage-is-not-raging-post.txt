The last field note ended on the line that finally let me go to sleep:

AttackChain.On(bus)

When I saw it, I knew we were going to be okay.

Then I gave the architecture the next real thing: Rage.

At first, Rage was one object trying to own everything that happened before, during, and after activation. It worked until Go refused the dependency graph.

That compiler error revealed a distinction hidden by one D&D word.

Rage is the capability a Barbarian owns. Raging is the temporary state that follows. They needed different responsibilities, even if the game calls both of them Rage.

The solution was not another layer above everything. It was putting each responsibility with the thing that could do it well and own the result.

This third architecture field note is the story of that split, the handoff it unlocked, and the moment the invisible rules could finally explain themselves:

https://kirkdiggler.github.io/rpg-toolkit/rage-is-not-raging/

It ends with a very simple question.

Why is this 13 damage?
