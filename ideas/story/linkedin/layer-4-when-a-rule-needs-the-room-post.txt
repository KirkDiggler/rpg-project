The last field note ended with a rule that could explain its own contribution.

Once Rage worked, the pattern started moving faster. New rules kept revealing the next reusable seam.

Then Dueling asked a different kind of question.

What is this character holding?

Sneak Attack widened the question to nearby allies. Protection needed the room, a shield, and an available reaction.

Conditions worked beautifully while they only needed their own state and the event in front of them. These rules needed to understand the world around that event.

I tried several places to put that knowledge. None fit until I realized it was runtime context for one publish, which is exactly what context.Context in Go is built to carry.

This fourth architecture field note is the story of that breakthrough:

https://kirkdiggler.github.io/rpg-toolkit/when-a-rule-needs-the-room/

It ends where the successful read model crossed a line.

A condition knew what should change. How could it ask the Character that owned the state to change it?
