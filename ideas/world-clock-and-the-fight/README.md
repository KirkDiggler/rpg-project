# The world's time while a fight runs

Idea, 2026-10-27. Kirk, after the slow-reserves investigation (rpg-project#661):
"monsters out of combat when combat is going on and there is a free roamer out
there. Can the free roamer expedite monsters to show up at the fight? The whole
place being frozen while a fight goes on and there is a free roamer also could
be a problem. We need to think through this and all the implications."

Both are true today, measured. `brainstorm.md` holds the model as the engine
runs it, six scenarios played on the castle harness with two player characters,
the candidate time models with what each fixes and breaks, and the questions.
The lane decision is rpg-project#683, "the world's time while a fight runs".

## The scene

Player A fights at the gate; the door is open; the reserves are in the barracks.
Player B is somewhere else in the castle, not in the fight.

- B stands still: the reserves walk six cells a fight round. Fine.
- B keeps walking: every pace B walks gives every creature outside the fight a
  full turn. At thirty cells a click, the reserves (and the hall guard, and the
  keep guard) move thirty cells per fight round.
- B walked ten paces before A started the fight, then stands still: the
  reserves do not move for ten fight rounds.
- B walks into the fight on round three: the freeze ends, B goes last.
- A clang in the fight: hearers hear it at once and act on it the next time the
  world's clock moves, which under the freeze is round eleven.
- B meets a guard in the kitchen: there is no second fight; B and the guard
  join A's initiative twenty-one cells away.

## Why

World time is the furthest-ahead player's time, not a sum and not the fight's.
A fight round only moves the world when a fighter is the one furthest ahead.
Every pace a roamer walks moves the world and hands every outside creature a
turn. Fight events are stamped with that time, so "within N rounds" rules
inside the fight age at the roamer's speed too.
